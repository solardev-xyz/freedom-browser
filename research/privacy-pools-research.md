Corrections and requirements from current docs:

- **Keys are signature-derived.** The [canonical flow](https://privacy-pools-v2-docs.vercel.app/concepts/keys) uses an EIP-712 payload and the SDK's two-stage HKDF to produce nullifying, revocable, and viewing keys. Preserve exact payload/derivation parameters and `revocableKeyIndex`. Do not replace this with PPv1's BIP-32 paths. Treat the internal signature as secret input capable of reproducing private keys, not an ordinary login signature: no renderer/page exposure or logging. Test deterministic signatures and independent restoration for each backend; the docs' repeatability claim is not a substitute for that test.
- **Hardware support is an open qualification question.** The blanket July assertion “Ledger cannot participate until v2.1” is not established by the current docs. ## 1. Mechanism

### The paper and the core idea

Buterin, Illum (Chainalysis), Nadler, Schär, Soleimani, *"Blockchain Privacy and Regulatory Compliance: Towards a Practical Equilibrium"* (Sept 2023), [SSRN 4563364](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4563364), published in *Blockchain: Research and Applications* (2024), [DOI 10.1016/j.bcra.2023.100176](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2096720923000519). Core idea: users withdraw with a zk proof of **membership in an association set** — a curated subset of deposits satisfying some policy — without revealing *which* deposit is theirs. Honest users can dissociate from illicit deposits; illicit depositors can't produce the proof. The paper frames this as a **separating equilibrium** between honest and dishonest users — privacy and compliance stop being adversaries.

### v1 in practice (0xbow, Ethereum mainnet since 2025-03-31)

- Deposit → 0xbow's **ASP** screens it (KYT via Chainalysis-style analytics) → approved deposits enter the association set → withdrawal proves set membership via Groth16, breaking the deposit↔withdrawal link within the approved set. ([The Block launch coverage](https://www.theblock.co/post/348959/0xbow-privacy-pools-new-cypherpunk-tool-inspired-research-ethereum-founder-vitalik-buterin), [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/privacy-pools-go-live-on-ethereum-with-vitalik-buterin-as-one-of-the-first-users) — Buterin was among the first depositors)
- **Ragequit:** a rejected (or voluntarily exiting) depositor can always recover funds to the *original depositing address*, publicly linked — funds are never seizable or frozen by the ASP, they just never gain privacy. This is the protocol's answer to "the screener is a custodian" arguments.
- Code: [`0xbow-io/privacy-pools-core`](https://github.com/0xbow-io/privacy-pools-core) — Yarn monorepo with `circuits` (Circom), `contracts` (Solidity), a reference `relayer`, and a TS `sdk`. **Apache-2.0.**
- Security: [Oxorio audit of v1](https://oxor-io.github.io/public_audits/Privacy%20Pools/Privacy%20Pools%20v1%20Audit%20Report.pdf); Groth16 **trusted-setup ceremony with 514 contributors** for the two v1 circuits (withdrawal + ragequit/exit), ceremony itself audited by Oxorio ([0xbow blog](https://0xbow.io/blog/celebrating-the-conclusion-of-the-trusted-setup-ceremony)).
- Funding/pedigree: $3.5M seed round; Ethereum Foundation collaboration ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/379395/0xbow-raises-3-5-million-seed-round-ethereum-foundation-backed-privacy-pools)); multi-asset began with Sky's USDS in July 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/363007/zero-knowledge-powered-privacy-pools-integrates-skys-usds-stablecoin-in-first-expansion-into-multi-asset-pools)).

### What's new in v2 (testnet since ~May/June 2026)

Sources: [v2 docs](https://privacy-pools-v2-docs.vercel.app/) (incl. `llms-full.txt`, which is the most complete single source), [what-is-pp-v2](https://privacy-pools-v2-docs.vercel.app/introduction/what-is-pp-v2), [EthCC9 interview with 0xbow, May 2026](https://medium.com/@crypto.diva/ethcc9-interview-with-0xbow-privacy-pools-compliant-onchain-privacy-cfec581d39a9).

v1 was a mixer-with-a-conscience: public deposit → private withdrawal. **v2 is a full shielded pool** — a UTXO/note system in which value can circulate privately:

| Area | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Model | deposit → withdraw only | **notes**: deposit → arbitrarily many **private in-pool transfers** (sender, recipient, amount all hidden) → withdraw |
| Amounts | pool-constrained | arbitrary note values; single pool |
| Assets | separate pools per asset | **single multi-asset pool** ("greater anonymity" — shared anonymity set across assets); ETH is tokenId `0xeeee…eeee` |
| Keys | monolithic note secrets | **three-key system**: nullifying, revocable, viewing keys; on-chain **Keystore** tree (depth 18, 262,144 accounts) with key rotation, "no separate seed phrase to back up" |
| Receiving | n/a | send to **any standard EVM address, no pre-registration** ("EVM address receipt"); registered accounts get *discoverable-mode* transfers (SDK auto-detects via keystore viewing key), unregistered fall back to out-of-band note delivery |
| Compliance | ASP approves deposits into association set; withdrawal proves membership | ASP screening is **gating**: a deposited note is `PENDING` (unspendable) until the ASP attests (`ASPRegistry.updateASPRoot(root, ipfsCID)`), then `ACTIVE`; `REJECTED` notes can only **ragequit** (label published on-chain, deposit publicly linked). Typical attestation "within an hour, though it can take up to 7 days"; per-asset **vetting fee (BPS)** ([deposit op](https://privacy-pools-v2-docs.vercel.app/operations/deposit)) |
| Disclosure | prove association set membership | **selective disclosure**: per-transaction receipts and viewing keys revealing account activity to a chosen party |
| Payments | n/a | **payment requests** — recipient-initiated, derived keypairs |
| Contracts | pool per asset | `Entrypoint` (asset config: enabled flag, min amount, vetting fee BPS, max relay fee) + `PoolVault` (state tree: LeanIMT depth 22, commitments + nullifiers) + `Keystore` + `ASPRegistry` + `PrivacyPoolRelay` (Sepolia V9 addresses in [deployments](https://privacy-pools-v2-docs.vercel.app/deployments/sepolia)) |
| Crypto | Groth16, Circom | same stack (Poseidon commitments, Groth16 via snarkjs); **v2 trusted-setup status not documented — verify before any mainnet integration** |
| Hardware support (September correction) | — | Current signature-based derivation requires signer-specific recovery and custody qualification; the earlier inference “no Ledger support until v2.1” is not established. |

The compliance mechanic got *stronger* in v2: illicit deposits never become spendable at all (vs v1 where they were excluded from the association set at withdrawal time). 0xbow's Mike McCabe at EthCC9: v1 was about *"validating whether the compliance mechanism actually works — and we believe it does"*; v2 aims to also be *"the best privacy protocol in the market overall."*

## 3. Integration surface

### SDK

- **Package:** `@privacy-pools-v2/sdk` — TypeScript, ~600 public names. **Not on npm**; early-access via private `v2-monorepo` workspace, gated on contacting 0xbow. **License unknown until access is granted** (flag; v1 core is Apache-2.0).
- **Shape:** `PoolSessionBuilder.fromConfig({ chainId, rpcUrl, ownerAddress, protocolKeys, aspUrl, relayers, walletInteractor: { type: "viem", walletClient } })` → `PoolSession` facade with `deposit`, `prepareTransfer`/`relayTransfer`, withdraw, keystore, ragequit sub-sessions. Note: **viem-first** wallet interactor — Freedom is ethers v6; the Kohaku doc already flags the "second web3 stack" cost, which persists here.
- **Proving:** client-side **Groth16 via snarkjs** (`ProofService` → `proveDeposit`/`proveTransact`/`proveRagequit`). Circuit artifacts (WASM + zkey + vkey) ship pre-compiled, loadable via `LocalCircuitArtifacts` (Node fs — right choice for Electron), `FetchCircuitArtifacts` (HTTP), or `IpfsCircuitArtifacts` (nice fit with Freedom's IPFS node). **Proving time and artifact sizes are undocumented** — must be measured in the spike; snarkjs Groth16 for trees this size is typically single-digit seconds on desktop, and zkeys plausibly tens of MB per circuit, but treat both as unknowns. In Electron, run proving in a **utility/worker process**, never the main process (event-loop blocking + memory spikes).
- **State sync:** note discovery scans `PoolVault.Note` events and trial-decrypts with the viewing key. Preferred source is the **ASP's HTTP data provider** (`/{chainId}/public/note-events` on `api-dev.0xbow.io`) — i.e. a 0xbow-hosted indexer; fallback is raw `eth_getLogs` paginated at 10k blocks ("slow on genesis scans"). Self-hosting the index is possible in principle (it's just events) but no packaged self-host indexer is documented. `NoteManager` persists notes + a `syncCursor` through a pluggable storage adapter — in Freedom this must live in **encrypted vault-adjacent storage** (private balances are as sensitive as keys; the Kohaku doc's point stands).
- **Relayer:** REST API — `POST /v1/quote/evm/{chainId}/transfer|receive` then `POST /v1/relay/evm/{chainId}/transfer|withdrawal`. Quotes are time-limited; fees quoted per operation; proofs are bound to the relayer's processor contract address. Trust model: the relayer **cannot steal funds** (proof-bound) but **sees the withdrawal recipient, timing, and your IP**, and can censor. This is exactly the call that should ride Freedom's Tor/Arti transport (or Nym for fire-and-forget relay submission) through the Outbound Services Gateway — relayer + ASP + note-sync endpoints all belong in the OSG registry as privacy-sensitive services, consistent with `privacy-roadmap.md` §4. Multiple relayer configs are supported (array), so BYO-relayer fits Freedom's BYO-endpoint policy.
- **Wallet requirements (corrected September 14):** public transactions use the existing signer and approval flow, while PPv2 protocol keys use its canonical EIP-712 signature/HKDF derivation. PPv1's `m/28784'/...` paths do not define v2 keys. Restrict the signature and resulting private material to the privileged runtime. Hardware/remote/contract-wallet support requires backend-specific recovery and custody tests; no v2.1 availability promise is established by the current docs.
- **Integration path (updated September 14):** preserve a protocol-adapter surface compatible with Kohaku. PPv1 can be a reference test; PPv2 remains the product target. Wrap the early-access v2 SDK directly when accessible if the Kohaku adapter remains absent. Public npm publication is not itself required for a spike, but reproducible dependency access and acceptable distribution rights are required.

## 4. Compliance angle

*(Facts and citations only; legal conclusions deliberately left to the maintainer — see §7.)*

**How the ASP model answers the Tornado problem.** Tornado Cash's legal vulnerability was structural: it could not exclude sanctioned/illicit flows, so ~all users shared an anonymity set with Lazarus. Privacy Pools inverts this: deposits are screened *before* gaining privacy (v2: before becoming spendable at all), illicit funds can only ragequit back to their tainted source address, and honest users can additionally make **selective disclosures** (receipts, viewing keys) to counterparties, auditors, or authorities. This is precisely the "practical equilibrium" of the 2023 paper, co-authored by a Chainalysis chief scientist — a design pedigree that matters rhetorically in any enforcement conversation.

**The 2025–26 landscape:**
- *Van Loon v. Treasury* (5th Cir., Nov 2024): immutable smart contracts are not "property" of a foreign national; OFAC exceeded its authority ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/treasury-department-delists-tornado-cash-following-the-fifth-circuits-decision.html)).
- **OFAC delisted Tornado Cash on 2025-03-21** ([Treasury press release](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0057); analyses: [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/04/a-legal-whirlwind-settles-treasury-lifts-sanctions), [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/crypto-policy-tracker/a-whirlwind-of-change-the-delisting-of-tornado-cash)). Treasury has since said lawful digital-asset users may use mixers for financial privacy.
- **DOJ policy**: stated end of "regulation by prosecution" against platforms/wallets/mixers for end-user conduct — yet the same DOJ **is seeking an October 2026 retrial of Roman Storm** on the hung money-laundering and IEEPA counts ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/392937/roman-storm-tornado-cash-retrial), [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/03/10/u-s-requests-october-retrial-for-tornado-cash-developer-roman-storm)).
- ***US v. Storm* status (as of 2026-07-08):** convicted Aug 2025 on one count of **conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money-transmitting business (18 U.S.C. §1960)**; jury hung on money-laundering and sanctions-conspiracy counts; Rule 29 acquittal motion argued 2026-04-09 and **still undecided**; retrial sought for Oct 2026 ([DeFi Education Fund tracker](https://www.defieducationfund.org/u-s-v-storm-2026-update/), [docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67720380/united-states-v-storm/)). The standing §1960 conviction is the single most relevant datapoint for anyone *operating* mixer-adjacent infrastructure with fees.
- **FinCEN**: the Oct 2023 §311 NPRM designating CVC mixing as a class of primary money-laundering concern was never finalized; status in 2026 unresolved. If finalized in anything like its proposed form it would impose reporting duties on covered *financial institutions* interacting with mixed funds — relevant to off-ramps Freedom users touch, not directly to Freedom.

**Browser vendor: built-in vs link-out (risk-shape sketch, not advice).**
- Freedom shipping *client-side software* — key derivation, proof generation, and UI run locally; 0xbow operates the ASP; third parties operate relayers — sits on the "publishing a tool" side of the line FinCEN's 2019 guidance draws for unhosted-wallet software (no acceptance-and-transmission of value, no custody). The Storm prosecution's §1960 theory leaned on operational control and fee-taking (relayer infrastructure, marketing, fee flows).
- Risk gradient, roughly: **link-out to privacypools.com** (lowest — comparable to bookmarking Uniswap) → **built-in UI over 0xbow ASP + third-party relayers** (moderate; vendor curates defaults, takes no fees, transmits nothing) → **operating a relayer or taking a fee on shield/unshield** (highest; closest to Storm's facts — avoid absent specific advice).
- Being *compliance-first* cuts both ways: it is the best available answer to "you shipped a mixer," and simultaneously a centralization/censorship story (a single ASP, 0xbow, decides whose deposits gain privacy; approval can take 7 days; policy is jurisdiction-configurable). Both halves belong in Freedom's honest copy.
- **EU/other:** AMLR (Reg. 2024/1624, applying 2027) prohibits *CASPs* from maintaining anonymity-enhancing arrangements; whether a non-custodial browser wallet is out of CASP/AMLR scope, and how MiCA treats it, is a §7 question. Netherlands: *Pertsev* (Tornado developer) conviction, appeal pending — the EU criminal-exposure benchmark. App-store distribution policies (if Freedom ever ships via stores) have historically been hostile to mixer UIs.

## 5. UX fit for a browser wallet

**Base journey (v2):**
1. **Shield**: deposit ETH/USDC from public wallet address → vetting fee → note `PENDING` (deposit publicly visible and linked to you) → ASP attestation (~1h, worst-case 7d) → `ACTIVE`. UI needs a pending state, a rejection path (ragequit explainer — "your funds come back to the same address, publicly"), and honest anonymity-set numbers.
2. **Accumulate/wait**: privacy grows with pool activity and time; amounts at the deposit and withdrawal edges remain public, so amount/timing correlation is the residual leak. UI should discourage same-amount same-day round-trips.
3. **Spend privately**: (a) **in-pool private transfer** to any EVM address (v2's headline — sender/recipient/amount hidden; discoverable if recipient is keystore-registered, out-of-band otherwise), or (b) **unshield via relayer to a fresh address** — relayer pays gas, so the fresh address needs no funding history at all. This "fresh address with no gas-funding trail" property is what nothing else in Freedom's stack provides.

**Composition with Freedom's existing ideas:**
- **Per-origin address derivation (`privacy-roadmap.md` §5.2.5) — the killer combo.** Per-origin addresses without private funding are cosmetic: funding them from the main wallet re-links everything on-chain. Funding them **from the pool via relayer** makes them genuinely unlinkable fresh identities per dApp. This turns §5.2.5 from "big product question" into a coherent architecture: vault → shield once → per-origin addresses drip-funded from the pool. Caveats: each funding event costs a relayer fee + on-chain gas, and its privacy is bounded by pool size; funding twenty origins with distinctive amounts in one hour is linkable. A sensible v1 of the feature funds origins lazily and in rounded denominations.
- **x402 micropayments: not directly payable from the pool.** x402's exact-EVM scheme is an EIP-3009 `transferWithAuthorization` signed by a public EOA (see `src/main/x402/client.js` — signTypedData over USDC); a facilitator settles it on-chain. There is no scheme where "payment" is a zk note-spend, and the recipient/facilitator would need protocol support that doesn't exist. **Realistic composition:** a pool-funded per-origin (or dedicated x402 burner) address holds a small public USDC budget and pays 402s normally — the payee sees a low-value burner, not your main wallet. That is privacy *from cross-site correlation*, not payee anonymity. v2's "payment requests" are conceptually adjacent to HTTP 402 but there is no bridge today; a "x402 settlement from shielded balance" scheme would be genuinely novel and is worth raising with 0xbow/x402 people. Also note roadmap §4's idea of x402-purchasing Nym zk-nym credentials — same burner-budget pattern.
- **Nym/Tor transports — complementary layers, clean split.** On-chain unlinkability (pool) and network unlinkability (transports) solve different halves of T1/T3. Concretely: deposit tx broadcast via Nym mixnet (already the roadmap's plan for tx broadcast); **ASP note-sync, relayer quote/relay calls, and `eth_getLogs` scans via Tor through the OSG** — otherwise the relayer and 0xbow's indexer learn IP↔note-set, recreating the RPC super-cookie problem one layer up. Freedom is unusually well-positioned here: no other wallet ships mixnet broadcast + onion-routed relayer calls + shielded pool as one coherent story.
- **Ledger (corrected September 14):** qualify EIP-712 repeatability/recovery and explain that derived private protocol keys are held by the application. Initially support software accounts; do not infer device-protected spending or a v2.1 timeline.
- **Vault/auto-lock**: note secrets and the note database are balance-revealing — store under the vault's encryption envelope, wipe on lock, same discipline as `vault-access.js`.

## 6. Alternatives check

- **Railgun** — live since 2021 on Ethereum, Polygon, Arbitrum, BSC; ~$5.16B cumulative private volume by June 2026 ([Baltex comparison](https://baltex.io/blog/ecosystem/aztec-vs-railgun-privacy-fees-2026-comparison)); shielded balances + private DeFi calls; its **Private Proofs of Innocence** are the same compliance idea as ASPs (prove non-membership in bad sets). Strengths: much larger anonymity set, mainnet-proven, Kohaku has a plugin. Weaknesses for Freedom: no Base/Gnosis either; token-governed protocol; POI list governance is less legible to a regulator than a named accountable ASP; heavier integration (its own SDK/engine). **Credible fallback if 0xbow v2 stalls; the Kohaku plugin surface keeps this swappable — which is exactly why the plugin architecture matters.**
- **Aztec** — full privacy L2 (Ignition chain live Nov 2025, Alpha mainnet Mar 2026, ~$1.2B TVL, [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/11/20/privacy-focused-aztec-network-s-ignition-chain-lights-up-on-ethereum)). Programmable privacy, impressive tech — but it's a *different chain*: bridging UX, separate fee market, Noir contracts. It doesn't make the user's **existing Ethereum/Base/Gnosis assets** private, which is Freedom's actual requirement. Watch; don't integrate as the wallet-privacy layer.
- **Stealth addresses (ERC-5564/6538)** — recipient-side unlinkability only: sender and amount stay public, and the funding-gas problem (paying gas from the stealth address without linking it) is unsolved without... a pool. Cheap to support, composes *with* Privacy Pools and with per-origin derivation (a per-origin address is functionally a self-generated stealth address), but it is not transactional privacy on its own.
- **Tornado-style (unscreened mixer)** — the strongest raw anonymity sets historically, and the exact legal fact pattern (Storm, Pertsev) a mainstream daily-driver browser must not default to, even post-delisting. Non-starter as a built-in.

**Why Privacy Pools is the right default for Freedom, if any is:** it is the only design whose *architecture* answers the regulator ("illicit deposits never gain privacy; users can selectively disclose"), whose paper trail runs through Buterin/EF/Chainalysis rather than through an indictment, and whose v2 note model matches a wallet's needs (arbitrary amounts, multi-asset, payment requests, EVM-address receive). The trade-off — a centralized ASP gatekeeper — is real, is mitigated by ragequit (self-custody is never conditional), and should simply be disclosed in Freedom's characteristically blunt copy.

## 7. Open legal questions for the maintainer (flagged, not answered)

1. **§1960 exposure boundary:** does bundling a shield/unshield UI with *default* relayer selection (vendor-curated list, no vendor fee, no vendor infrastructure) stay outside "money transmitting business"? Where exactly does the Storm §1960 theory (as it survives the Rule 29 ruling and any retrial) place a non-custodial software vendor? Watch: Failla's acquittal ruling (pending) and the Oct 2026 retrial.
2. **Fee question:** any Freedom revenue share on relayer fees or vetting fees would change the analysis materially — decide *policy* (recommend: never) before design.
3. **EU:** is Freedom (non-custodial, no service relationship) outside CASP scope under MiCA/AMLR 2024/1624, and does the AMLR anonymity-instrument prohibition (from 2027) reach *software vendors* shipping shielded-pool UIs? German/EU distribution is presumably Freedom's home jurisdiction — this is the one to nail down first.
4. **OFAC recidivism risk:** delisting was discretionary, post-*Van Loon*; a future administration could re-designate a specific pool contract (litigation risk, not prohibition). What's the contingency if 0xbow contracts are designated while users hold notes? (Ragequit works on-chain regardless — but is a UI that executes a ragequit from a sanctioned contract itself a prohibited dealing?)
5. **ASP reliance:** any diligence duty on 0xbow's screening policy (it is jurisdiction-configurable)? If 0xbow's ASP admits deposits later shown illicit, does the vendor's "compliance-first" marketing create representations risk?
6. **FinCEN §311 NPRM:** if finalized, do reporting duties on financial institutions handling "mixed" CVC create practical de-banking risk for users unshielding to exchange deposit addresses — and does Freedom owe users a warning UI?
7. **Selective disclosure + law enforcement:** should Freedom build the viewing-key/receipt export UX (user-controlled disclosure) as a deliberate compliance affordance? Probably yes technically — but the framing (who Freedom suggests users disclose to) is a legal-comms question.
