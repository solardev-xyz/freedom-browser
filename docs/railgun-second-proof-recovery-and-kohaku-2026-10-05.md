# Railgun interrupted proof recovery and sequential Kohaku operations

Twenty-five native Electron processes pass on one 928-source inventory: four four-process recovery cases, two same-process sequential Kohaku cases and seven compatibility processes. Every original driver exited 0 and was drained. Both initial note histories (Shield and Transact) and acknowledged/lost-response submission outcomes pass. This extends qualification fixtures; production policy, dependencies, activation and APIs are unchanged.

## Original-signature recovery across four processes

1. Setup makes the first partial withdrawal, normally scans its change and persists the combined POI attempt with disposable signed list acceptance.
2. A fresh process stages that actual Transact change and commits the second genuine private signature. The fixture substitutes a refusal only after the real broker has persisted the signature. Production reports signed-unfinished; the process drains without making the proof or an Ethereum transaction signature.
3. Another process reopens the two authentic encrypted records and invokes the production proof-recovery host. It reconstructs from the original second capsule/signature, independently verifies the resulting proof, and fills only that record's empty proof slot. A repeated recovery returns proof-present with no further measured work or file changes. No new private signature, eligibility lookup or submission occurs here.
4. A final process reopens the saved proof and calls ordinary recovered submission. Fresh wallet, creator, TXID, verifier, list/root, preflight, disclosure, transaction review and journal gates run before one Ethereum signature/send. Normal terminal ingestion spends the selected change while preserving unrelated notes and balances.

The final submission process alone marks the composed signed-unfinished recovery chain qualified. Earlier-stage and nested component flags retain their narrower scope. The preserved-original-capsule/signature/proof/hold flag refers to the first operation; the second operation intentionally fills its previously empty proof slot.

Handoffs contain hashes and public run/profile/source metadata, not plaintext capsules, private signatures, proof calldata, owned notes, service signing keys or reusable receipts. Every predecessor PID must be absent and every profile/runtime/source/predecessor hash must match. The next process uses the immediately preceding encrypted snapshot.

The lost-response fixture verifies the signed transaction, nonce and durable attempted journal before throwing its identified transport error. Production returns uncertainty; reconciliation and terminal ingestion use the existing attempt without resending. Duplicate submission refuses before and after reconciliation, with exactly two logical pooled-RPC handle releases per refusal and no additional requests, reviews, credentials or signing.

## Storage and exact activity

P1 uses 16 utilities, 6 storage workers and 6 utility credential loans. The deliberate post-commit refusal produces one attempted but unadmitted operate result and a controlled session revocation. It reads the public gas-paying address once; it does not sign or send an Ethereum transaction.

P2 uses 7 utilities, 3 workers and 4 loans, including identity bootstrap. Its first completed-wallet RPC lazily creates one pooled transport. Every other service counter remains unchanged during recovery; source release and transport closure occur in outer cleanup. There is no new spending-sign, private-operate, POI/TXID lookup or Ethereum signing job. Identity bootstrap still derives the spending public identity; this is not a claim that no spending-derived material is used.

During the measured recovery call, exactly two encrypted account files change: capsule proof storage and its matching sequence floor. Across the whole cold process, seven account files change because authenticated bootstrap also updates lifecycle metadata. All other snapshot classes and filenames remain unchanged. The assertion-only observer checks proposed logical transitions; authenticated postcommit reads, exact file-hash comparison and the next independent process reopening the proof corroborate persistence. This is not a claim about ciphertext correctness, fsync, physical writes or rollback durability.

P3 uses 23 utilities, 7 workers and 5 loans, with one journaled Ethereum signature/send and no new private signature/proof. First-operation canonical observation refreshes are one in P1, zero in P2 and three in P3. Immutable first-operation identity and intent remain bound through those legitimate journal updates.

## Two actual Kohaku instances

Separate same-process runs create a real first plugin for a partial withdrawal, close and drain it, normally scan the change, then create a distinct real plugin for a full withdrawal of that change. Both initial histories pass. Observers record two proofs, one receiver and two submissions; the second full withdrawal creates no change receiver. Copied, unregistered, consumed and previous-consumed-instance tokens refuse without extra measured work. This last control does not claim coverage of an unconsumed foreign live token.

Shield-first takes 103,970ms with 316 utilities/33 workers/23 loans; Transact-first takes 113,594ms with 359/36/25. The actual first and second amounts, note/nullifier identities, stored capsules and proved calldata are joined. Terminal ingestion and unrelated-balance preservation follow the second submission. These acknowledged same-process adapter runs are separate from the four-process direct-controller recovery cases; no claim is made that a Kohaku operation token survives restart.
